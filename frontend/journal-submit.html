What we accept

Poem

Verse in any form, classical or free. Keep each line of verse on its own line.

8 to 40 lines.

Short story

Fiction with a beginning, a middle and an end, told in clear Arabic.

Up to about 2,000 words.

Essay

An argued piece on a subject that matters to you, with its sources credited.

500 to 1,500 words.

Reflection

A short, personal piece of thought or feeling.

100 to 500 words.

Translation

A piece you have translated into or from Arabic. Name the original author and work, and credit the source.

Up to about 1,500 words, with the source credited.

Research paper

A study with a clear question, evidence and references. Attach a Word document for long papers.

Any length. Attach a Word document if it is longer than the text box allows.

We prefer concise, carefully edited writing. These are guides rather than hard limits. The only fixed limit is 20,000 characters of pasted text. If a piece seems to need more space, it often needs more editing first.

Guidelines

Your own work

Send only work you wrote or translated yourself. Plagiarism is never accepted, and anything quoted must be credited to its source.

Sound Arabic

We may suggest corrections to spelling, grammar and wording before publishing.

In line with our standards

Writing on religion follows the Sunni scholarly tradition. Quote Qurʼānic verses and ḥadīth accurately and name the source.

Respectful

No insults, no mockery of religion, no partisan attacks, and nothing that targets a person or a group.

Published elsewhere?

We do not turn work down for that reason alone. Say so in your bio, with where and when it appeared.

Your bio

A bio of up to 200 words is required. Describe your personality, not your résumé, and do not try to sell yourself. Praise and exaggeration will be edited out.

What we look for

Everyday life

The small moments people walk past: a street at dawn, a kitchen, a school bus, a neighbour's greeting. Show us the detail others missed.

Clear, plain Arabic

Simple and precise beats ornate. We value clarity and sound language over heavy decoration, but plain should never mean flat.

A light touch of humour

Wit is welcome, used like salt in a meal: a little, never a spoonful. Do not force it.

Timeless over topical

We lean towards what people have always shared: family, friendship, patience, loss and gratitude. A current issue is fine if you find the personal, human side of it.

Faith lived, not lectured

Writing from a Muslim life is welcome: a dawn prayer, a lesson from a teacher, a verse that stayed with you. Show it through experience instead of preaching.

Fresh and original

We would rather read a new idea in plain words than a cliché in fine ones.

Before you send

Ask yourself:

  • Could I cut a fifth of it without losing the meaning?
  • Does every line or paragraph add something new: an image, a turn, a fact?
  • Does it end before it starts explaining itself?
  • I have read it aloud and corrected the mistakes I found.
  • It is in Arabic, every line of verse is on its own line, and anything I quoted is credited.
  • I used an email address I check, and if the piece is long I have attached a Word document.
