Murajaah: how to keep the Qur'an you have memorised

Finishing your hifdh is a milestone, not the finish line. Here is how steady revision keeps every juz firm.

Himaayah Schools · 24 October 2026 · 2 min read

Finishing the memorisation of the Qur'an is one of the greatest milestones a student can reach. But every hafidh soon learns that memorisation is only half of the work. What is not revised slowly fades, and what is revised often becomes firm enough to recite in salah without hesitation.

Why revision matters more after hifdh

While you are memorising, revision happens naturally: you repeat the new page, then the pages before it. Once the last page is done, that daily rhythm can disappear. Weeks pass without opening the early juz, and small mistakes creep in, a skipped word here, a mixed-up ending there. Murajaah is the habit that stops this from happening.

A simple structure that works

Most teachers divide revision into three parts. You do not need all three every day at the start, but over a week each should get its turn.

  • Recent revision: the portions you have worked on most recently, read every day until they are smooth.
  • Rotating revision: a fixed amount of older portions each day, for example one or two juz, so the whole Qur'an is covered in a set cycle.
  • Listening and reciting: reciting what you revise in your salah, and listening to a trusted reciter to correct your pronunciation and rhythm.

Choose a cycle you can keep

The best plan is the one you can keep every day. Revising one juz a day completes the Qur'an in a month. Two juz a day completes it in about two weeks. If a day is busy, keep the habit with a smaller portion rather than skipping it entirely.

Recite to someone

Revising alone is useful, but reciting to a teacher shows you mistakes you cannot hear yourself. A teacher notices where you hesitate, which similar verses you confuse, and which portions need more time. That is why a teacher checking your recitation regularly makes such a difference.

Signs your revision is working

  • You can begin a portion from the middle of a page without looking.
  • You rarely confuse similar verses in different surahs.
  • You can recite long portions in salah calmly.
