Diagnostic Assessment

Know who this child is becoming, not just what they've scored.

Before any student is placed at Himaayah Schools, they go through a diagnostic assessment. It isn't a test to pass or fail — it's how we learn what a student already knows, how they learn best, where their natural strengths lie, and which pathway will actually help them thrive. Every family leaves with a written report and a personalised learning plan.

Why We Assess

Six questions, one full picture.

We don't rank students or label them as intelligent or unintelligent. Every assessment exists to answer six questions that shape everything that follows.

How It Works

From first interview to a plan you can follow.

The full assessment runs across a few sessions, not one long sitting, so no single day defines the outcome.

1

Parent Interview

We ask about expectations, concerns, home habits, and how you'd describe your child's personality — quiet, curious, talkative, analytical, and everything between.

2

Student Interview

A one-on-one conversation about what the student enjoys, what they struggle with, and what they'd become if nobody judged the decision.

3

Assessment Sessions

Academic, cognitive, learning style, executive function, creativity, and communication tasks, each completed directly with our assessors.

4

The Learning Plan

Every finding is combined into a written Individual Learning Plan with immediate, medium-term, and long-term goals.

5

Consultation Meeting

Results are discussed together with the student and parents, and a recommended pathway is proposed before enrolment.

The Outcome

An Individual Learning Plan, not a report card.

Every admitted student receives a personalised plan built directly from their assessment, reviewed with parents and revisited every six to eight weeks.

0 – 3 Months

Immediate Goals

Specific academic and behavioural targets the student can act on right away, alongside any literacy or numeracy support needed from day one.

3 – 12 Months

Medium-Term Goals

Skill development, subject improvement, and enrichment, with home strategies for parents and classroom strategies for teachers.

1 – 5 Years

Long-Term Aspirations

Educational and career planning aligned with the student's demonstrated strengths, revisited as the student grows.

We do not begin with the question, "What career does this child want?" We begin with a more important one: "Who is this child becoming?" Only once we understand the whole learner do we recommend a pathway.
